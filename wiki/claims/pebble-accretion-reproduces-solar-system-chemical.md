## Statement

Planetesimal formation by streaming instability followed by rapid accretion of drifting pebbles during the protoplanetary disk lifetime can reproduce many of the Solar System's chemical and isotopic observables, including early planetesimal chronology, volatile depletion patterns, and the mixed inner/outer disk signatures of rocky planets.

## Evidence summary

Supporting:

- [[cosmochemistry-planetary-systems]] reviews multiple independent constraints: early differentiated asteroids, later chondrite parent bodies, rapid Mars accretion, NC-CC nucleosynthetic structure, volatile depletion relative to CI chondrites, and thermal processing during pebble drift.
- The same review argues that pebble accretion naturally moves volatile and organic delivery into the main rocky-planet growth phase, rather than making it depend only on late stochastic impacts.

Qualifying:

- Pebble accretion and collisional growth are not mutually exclusive.
- Volatile-driven processing and core formation can modify isotope systems, so not every tracer gives a direct source mixture.
- The hydrodynamics of sublimated volatiles in proto-atmospheres remains an active simulation problem.

## Conditions and scope

This claim is strongest for Solar System chronology and cosmochemical pattern matching. It is weaker when generalized to all exoplanetary systems, where detailed isotope measurements are unavailable and observations mostly constrain mass, radius, host-star abundances, and atmospheres.

## Counter-evidence

The classical collisional accretion model remains viable for parts of terrestrial planet growth, especially for late-stage giant impacts and Moon-forming scenarios. Some Hf-W and mantle isotope interpretations have historically been used to argue for slower Earth growth, though the review notes these signals may be reset or modified by later equilibration.

## Open questions

- Which isotope systems can separate source mixing from volatility-driven processing during pebble accretion?
- How much volatile material is recycled back to the disk versus retained in proto-atmospheres?
- Can exoplanet atmosphere observations distinguish early pebble-delivered volatiles from late impact delivery?
