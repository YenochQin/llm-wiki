## Statement

Highly charged ions can enhance tests of fundamental physics because their relativistic electronic structure and compact orbitals amplify sensitivity to bound-state QED, variation of constants, local Lorentz invariance, and possible new forces.

## Evidence summary

Supporting:

- [[highly-charged-ions-optical-clocks-applications]] reports that some HCI transitions have much larger alpha-variation sensitivity than established Yb+ clock transitions.
- The review notes that hyperfine HCI clocks are sensitive to alpha, the electron-to-proton mass ratio, and nuclear `g` factors.
- It also describes HCI as promising systems for SME Lorentz-violation searches and isotope-shift King-plot tests of new boson-mediated forces.

Qualifying:

- Enhanced sensitivity is useful only when systematic shifts and nuclear/atomic-theory uncertainties are controlled.
- Several proposals remain at the candidate or near-future experimental stage.

## Conditions and scope

The claim is strongest for selected candidate ions such as Ir17+, Cf15+/Cf17+, hydrogenlike hyperfine systems, and HCI states with large Lorentz-violation matrix elements. It is weaker as a blanket claim over all HCI.

## Counter-evidence

Existing neutral and singly charged-ion clocks already reach extremely high stability and accuracy. HCI must overcome production, cooling, spectroscopy, and theory challenges before their higher sensitivity translates into stronger bounds.
