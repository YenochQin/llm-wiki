## Statement

Highly charged ion optical clocks can suppress several systematic shifts that limit conventional ion clocks, especially polarizability- and electric-quadrupole-related shifts, because compact HCI orbitals reduce their coupling to external fields.

## Evidence summary

Supporting:

- [[highly-charged-ions-optical-clocks-applications]] argues that HCI size scales down with charge, reducing dipole and quadrupole moments and polarizabilities by one or more orders of magnitude.
- The same review describes cancellation approaches such as Zeeman averaging, quadrupole-shift averaging, and magic-drive operation.
- Hyperfine HCI clock proposals are summarized as having extremely small electric and magnetic field shifts, with motional and collisional shifts left as main practical concerns.

Qualifying:

- Fine-structure and level-crossing candidates vary substantially; some require linear-Zeeman and quadrupole cancellation.
- Many candidates lack complete atomic data for a proper clock uncertainty budget.

## Conditions and scope

This claim applies to candidate classes with enough atomic data to estimate systematic shifts. It should not be read as saying every HCI is automatically a better clock than Al+, Yb+, or Sr lattice clocks.

## Counter-evidence

HCI can be more sensitive to micromotion and electric fields through their larger charge, and state preparation/readout are harder than for established clock ions. Complex electronic structure can also increase second-order Zeeman or tensorial shifts.
