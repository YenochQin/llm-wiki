## Definition

Pebble accretion cosmochemistry is the use of chemical, isotopic, and chronological constraints from Solar System materials to test whether rocky planets formed by rapid accretion of drifting mm- to cm-scale solids after planetesimals formed by streaming instability.

## Intuition

If rocky planets grow mostly by local collisions among large embryos, their building blocks should be dominated by nearby inner-disk material and late volatile delivery should matter strongly. If they grow by pebble accretion, drifting solids from multiple disk regions can contribute during the main accretion phase, and thermal processing during that drift becomes a primary control on volatile inventories, isotope mixtures, and atmosphere-building material.

## Formal notation

No single equation defines the concept. The usual observables are mixture fractions and time constraints:

```text
planet composition ~= f(inner NC reservoir, outer CC/CI-like reservoir, thermal processing, core-mantle-atmosphere partitioning)
growth time <= protoplanetary disk lifetime
```

In practice, isotope systems such as Ti, Cr, Mo, Si, Zn, Zr, Hf-W, Al-Mg, and Pb-Pb act as constraints on source reservoirs, accretion age, and processing.

## Variants

- **Streaming-instability planetesimal formation**: concentrates solids into ~100 km bodies, with early differentiated asteroids and later chondrite parent bodies reflecting different disk epochs.
- **Pebble-driven terrestrial growth**: planetary embryos grow by accreting drifting solids within a few Myr, potentially explaining rapid Mars formation and volatile acquisition.
- **Thermally processed pebble accretion**: inward-drifting solids lose or redistribute volatile species as disk temperature and proto-atmosphere recycling change.
- **Isotope-gradient interpretation**: NC and CC reservoirs may record a radial compositional gradient plus transport rather than perfectly isolated inner and outer reservoirs.

## Comparison

Compared with classical collisional growth, pebble accretion emphasizes disk-lifetime growth, radial material flux, and early volatile delivery. Compared with purely dynamical planet-formation models, the cosmochemical version is constrained by measured meteorite and planetary isotope systems rather than architecture alone.

## When to use

- when explaining Solar System isotope anomalies as constraints on rocky-planet formation;
- when connecting meteorite chronology to disk-lifetime planet growth;
- when reasoning about volatile delivery during main accretion rather than late impacts;
- when extrapolating Solar System cosmochemistry to small exoplanet composition and primordial atmospheres.

## Known limitations

- Many isotope systems are affected by secondary processing, including volatility-driven loss, core formation, and giant impacts.
- Solar System constraints are high precision but single-system; they may not represent typical rocky-planet formation.
- Hydrodynamic treatment of sublimated volatiles during pebble accretion is still developing.
- Exoplanet observations cannot yet validate the detailed composition predictions for individual rocky planets.

## Open problems

- Quantify the radial flux of outer-disk material into terrestrial-planet accretion zones.
- Determine whether the NC-CC structure is a dichotomy or a compositional gradient.
- Identify isotope systems robust to thermal processing during pebble drift and accretion.
- Link JWST observations of inner-disk volatiles to final rocky-planet volatile budgets.

## My understanding

This concept is the "composition-aware" version of pebble accretion. The mechanism is not just fast growth; the scientifically useful part is that it makes concrete predictions about what isotopes, volatile depletion patterns, and early atmospheres should look like if drifting pebbles were the dominant feedstock.
